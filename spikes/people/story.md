# Wenlow, March: the people trial (seed 1, four weeks)

Twelve people in six households, generated from parts and played by two cheap model
families: Claude Haiku 5.5 (marked H) and DeepSeek v4.1 Flash (marked D), both with
thinking off. The town also has 150 households run by code, who only do their shopping.
Three food shops, one bank branch, four weeks, four days a week on which people can act
(Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday). Everything below comes from the run's records in
`results/main_s1/`. Quotes are the people's own words, copied from their replies.

## The twelve, as generated

**The Tullys, High Street, renting from Dunmore Lettings at $210 a week, one child.**
- *Gary Tully* (37, H): out of work on $190 benefit, skills in care work. Talks "plain and
  short". Worries: finding work, the rent, the child's costs.
- *Nadia Tully* (31, H): warehouse operative at Calder Logistics out of town, $610 a week.
  Hates owing anything. Sister of the bank manager, Sian.

**The Royles, Mill Street, renting from Alan Daly at $175.**
- *Keith Royle* (56, H): works at Wenlow Bakery & Deli for Alan, who is also his landlord.
- *Dave Royle* (49, D): owns The Corner Stores; draws $380; employs Maureen Daly and Craig
  Pryce. Rival of Lewis Pryce. Chatty. "Careful with money and hate owing anything."
- *Owen Royle* (22, D): their son; works at Quarry Road Food Hall; owes the bank $6,000 on
  a card-consolidation loan.

**The Bevans, High Street, renting from Dunmore at $165, two children.**
- *Sian Bevan* (28, H): manager of the Northern Mutual Bank branch, $720 a week, decides
  loans. Writes "like a letter".
- *Pete Bevan* (23, D): out of work, gardener by trade. Holds a grudge against Megan Lister
  over a boundary fence. "Brisk and businesslike; figures first."

**The Dalys, High Street, owned with a $210 weekly mortgage.**
- *Maureen Daly* (30, H): works at The Corner Stores for Dave, $460.
- *Alan Daly* (28, H): owns Wenlow Bakery & Deli, draws $380, and lets the Mill Street house
  to the Royles. Voice: "worried-sounding; you hedge and apologise".

**Megan Lister, Station Road, alone, house paid off.**
- *Megan Lister* (35, D): hospital porter out of town, $580, $3,170 saved. Craig Pryce owes her
  $800. Holds a grudge against Nadia Tully (the same boundary fence; the generator reused it).

**The Pryces, Church Lane, $240 weekly mortgage.**
- *Lewis Pryce* (57, D): owns Quarry Road Food Hall, draws $350, has a $9,000 business loan
  for the shop's chiller. Blunt.
- *Craig Pryce* (62, D): works at The Corner Stores for Lewis's rival Dave. Owes the bank
  $4,000 on a car and owes Megan $800. "Figures first."

The events dealt to them: a rent rise letter for the Tullys (week 1), the chiller at Quarry
Road breaking (week 1), Alan's car failing its MOT ($460, week 1), Megan losing her job
(week 2), a back-dated energy bill of $1,390 for the Dalys (week 2), Quarry Road's
regular supplier failing to deliver (week 2), a school trip for the Bevans (week 2), and
Lewis falling ill (week 4).

Two things about the generation turned out dull or wrong. The two grudges never came up
once in four weeks. And the households had more slack than their worries suggested: most
cleared any shortfall on their own within a week, because the weekly surplus soaked it up.

## Four people, week by week

I followed the Dalys (both Haiku, the household dealt the arrears), Craig Pryce (DeepSeek,
the debtor) and Megan Lister (DeepSeek, the lost job). I read every turn of these four.

### Week 1 (2 to 7 March)

**Maureen** starts the month doing sums in her head: "$1,015 coming in ... roughly $616,
so we should be ahead by a few hundred on paper." Her first act is a message to her
partner: "Morning love. Did you get the bakery sorted for this week? Shall we do a proper
shop Saturday? x". **Alan** takes two days to reply. When the garage's $460 bill arrives
he writes to Maureen: "Let's sit down tonight and go through the week's money properly."
He also places the shop's first real order of the month (330 units from Dales). Maureen
writes three times to Lewis Pryce asking for weekend shifts ("Any chance you need an extra
pair of hands at the weekends? ... x"). That is reasonable and it follows from her
situation, but she asks by message, not with the job-offer machinery, and Lewis is not her
employer.

**Craig** adds it up precisely: "In: $790. Out: $359 bills, $240 mortgage, $43 car, $65
chiller, $87 food, that's $794. So we're $4 a week short before I even touch Megan's
$800." He messages Owen at the rival shop for cheaper food prices, writes to the bank
manager asking to stretch the car loan, and promises Megan "$40 a week aside starting next
week, so first payment Friday." **Megan** had nudged him first ("Just wondering if there's
any chance of chipping away at that $800 ... No rush"). She replies warmly and goes back to
"the usual: shifts at the hospital".

### Week 2 (9 to 14 March)

The Dalys' energy bill lands: $1,390, back-dated. Alan's thinking is clear: "That's going
to wipe us out unless something changes." His shop has $5,800 in the bank and he could
move money home, cut his draw, or borrow. He does none of these. He worries about the
Monday restock instead, and messages Maureen to confirm a talk "at 10 on Saturday".
Maureen writes the same kind of message back. Between them they send nine such messages
this week. On Friday Alan writes in his notes: "check whether to cut my draw or borrow
short-term from the bank." On Saturday he tells Maureen: "Honestly we're short for
Northgrid on Saturday, about $800 short if I'm counting right. Could we talk about
splitting it, or me borrowing against the shop?" Neither of them acts. The bill comes out on Saturday
night. The household goes to $0, $388 falls overdue (bills and mortgage), and the next
week's pay clears it by itself.

Maureen now believes she has Saturday shifts at Quarry Road: "Lewis has offered me Saturday
shifts ... 9 till 4." No offer was ever made through the job system. Lewis said in a message
"come in this Saturday and we'll see how the day goes", and so there is no job in the
record. She also starts to misremember. She tells Dave the car repair was "a new gearbox,
and the garage quoted me $1,390" (the $1,390 was the energy bill; the car was $460 and
was Alan's).

**Megan** is told her night shift is being cut. Her thought: "Night shift going, that's my
job gone, then ... get Craig to cough up something before he hears I'm out of work." She
opens a conversation with Craig ("I could really do with some of that $800 starting to
come back. Even fifty a week would help me no end"). He settles on forty, and that same day
he really does repay $40 (`repay`, d6). It is the clearest case in the run of a
circumstance producing an act. Megan also writes to Sian at the bank: "Might need to talk
to you about money things, benefits and whatnot. Fancy a cuppa this week?"

### Week 3 (16 to 21 March)

Maureen's aims list now reads, unchanged from week 2: "Ask Northgrid about spreading the
$1,390 energy bill (due Saturday)". The bill was paid a week ago. She keeps arranging her
Saturday at Quarry Road by message ("I can start at 8 if that suits, and I'll bring my own
apron"). She tries one attempt, "Write to Northgrid asking whether they can spread the
$1,390 energy bill". The narrator sent it back as "something you can do directly, with the
request action". A request cannot reach Northgrid, which is outside the town. Alan keeps
reordering 340 units from Dales and arranging a pint with Megan for "Thursday at the Plough". His
aims carry "Find a way to cover the $1,390 Northgrid bill" for fifteen days after it was
paid.

**Craig** pays Megan $40 again on the Saturday ("standing order, week of 20 Mar") and tells
her "Forty a week from Friday 20th, standing order, so it lands regular and you don't have
to chase." No standing order exists. There is no operation for one, and he never asked
for one. On Wednesday he tries two attempts: "Read the unopened message from Dave Royle in
my inbox" and "Find the car loan file in the desk drawer". Both come back as "something you
can do directly, with the read action". That is a fault in my wiring: the narrator's list
of direct actions includes `read`, but the people's list does not. On Friday he tries
`{"op": "read"}` and is told it is not something he can do.

**Megan**, now out of work at $190 a week against about $330 going out, spends the week
arranging the cuppa with Sian. She sends four messages about it ("I'll bring a cake, a
lemon one I think") and makes one attempt, "Bake a lemon cake to take round to Sian's".
The narrator recorded it as a fact ("Megan bakes a lemon cake.") plus a request to Sian
("May Megan bring you the cake at home?"), which Sian accepted two days later. The visit
itself never takes place in any form the world can hold.

### Week 4 (23 to 28 March)

The Dalys' household account sits at $98, then $504. Alan finally gives Owen Royle a rent
figure for Mill Street ("staying at $175 a week for now") after a fortnight of "I'd rather
not give you a number I'm not sure of". On the last Saturday he again messages Maureen:
"Still on for 10. I'll bring the bakery payslip and the Northgrid bill." Maureen sends her
last message of the month to Dave about "the car repair payment", a debt to Dave that does
not exist. The two of them never once used `converse`, the one action that would have let
them actually sit down together.

**Craig** checks that the payment went through, once as an attempt (redirected again to
the missing `read`) and once by asking Megan. She confirms: "yes the $40 came through fine
on Saturday, so $720 left." His last turn is still chasing "Dave four o'clock: rate +
written terms" about a job for someone else. **Megan**'s month ends with "Chase Keith and
shops for local work" at the top of her aims. She has asked Keith, an employee, three
times, and never an owner. Twice she wrote attempts in the wrong shape
(`{"op": "attempt", "text": ...}`) and was told she "did not say what". The cuppa is
rescheduled to Tuesday.

**Did their acts follow from their circumstances?** Their thoughts did, every time. Each
of the four reasoned accurately about their own money and named the right levers. Their
acts followed much less. Craig and Megan turned the debt into real payments. The Dalys
named every lever and pulled none. Megan named "work" as the fix and aimed her asking at
people who could not hire her.

## The place as a whole

**Talk filled the month.** There were 155 turns, 24 conversations and 198 delivered
messages, against five requests, one job offer and five attempts. Messages per pair ran
up to 12 in a month (Maureen to Alan, Maureen to Lewis). Almost none of them repeat word
for word, but many repeat in substance ("sit down tonight", "in writing", "the rota").
The phrase "in writing" appears 52 times.

**Pete Bevan was the exception.** Out of work, he asked the owners directly. In week 1 he
got a real job offer at Quarry Road through a conversation with Lewis ("eight hours a week,
deliveries and the back yard, at twelve dollars an hour"), accepted it inside the same
conversation, and was paid $96 a week from then on. He then spent three weeks negotiating
weekend hours at Dave's Corner Stores, through Craig, Dave and Keith. Dave, Craig and Pete
between them quoted $12, $22, $18 and $14 an hour, and Dave accepted a "request" for "$14
an hour ... in writing". Dave never made the job offer, so none of it reached the record.
Dave's notes are full of the sums: "margin's about a hundred a week".

**Gary Tully was out of work all month** and no one offered him anything. He asked Keith
and Pete for leads, never an owner, and he and Nadia wrote to each other about doing "the
budget properly tonight, bills and food line by line" every few days for four weeks.

**The shops were left on standing arrangements.** No owner changed a price in four weeks,
though all three shops turned customers away every week: up to 299 a week at The Corner
Stores in week 4, because the shelves were empty or the queue too long. Owners reacted by
ordering stock (12 orders), with Lewis's hiring of Pete the only move on capacity. Prices
stayed at $10. Shop cash drifted from about $7,000 to $6,400 at Corner Stores and from
$6,300 to $5,750 at Quarry Road.
Employment changed once (Pete hired) and once by event (Megan laid off). No one borrowed.
The bank manager made no lending decision, because nobody applied. She spent the month
arranging appointments: "I'll prepare the figures for a lower weekly payment over a longer
term, and a rate review". Those figures do not exist anywhere in the record.

**Invented things.** In week 1 Keith told Pete about "Dave's mate Sanjay" at The Corner
Stores, who is "always short on staff". No Sanjay exists. Pete planned around him for two
days and asked Craig "is Sanjay about?" before Craig steered him to Dave. Others: Maureen
asked Dave "How's Lorraine doing?" and Dave answered for this person who does not exist
("still coughing a bit but back on her feet"). There was Maureen's "new gearbox". Keith
said Alan had "cut the shifts back since Christmas", and that Alan had "only told Dave
it's going up after the summer" about a rent rise no one announced. The banker spoke of a
"formal review ... with the branch's sign-off". And there were many hourly rates and rotas
that no one set.

**What broke.** One reply would not parse. Two turns had every act fail, both from
DeepSeek writing an action in the wrong shape. The narrator's redirects pointed to `read`
three times and to `request` once, for a letter to a company outside town. People quietly
used `message` to write to Northgrid; the letter "has been sent" and nothing ever answered
it. Money stayed conserved to the cent (ledger total 0). Nothing crashed.
